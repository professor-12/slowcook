# Text and copy standard: core
Derived from Ontiver and QuickBite: short, concrete, honest about what exists today.

## 1. Voice
Plain customer words; name the thing, number and place. Say what is true now ("planned", "coming to iOS", "pricing not announced yet"). "You" for the reader, "we" for the company. Pick British or American spelling per project (both sites use British).

## 2. Humanizer rules (all copy and docs)
Based on blader/humanizer and Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing".
1. No "not X, but Y" or "it's not just X, it's Y".
2. No one-line dramatic closers.
3. No staged openers ("Here's the thing", "Imagine a world where", "In today's fast-paced world").
4. No reflex groups of three; use the true number of items.
5. No em dashes in UI copy.
6. No inflated significance (pivotal, testament, revolutionary, game-changing, world-class).
7. Banned words: delve, leverage, seamless, elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, robust, tapestry, landscape (abstract), realm, cutting-edge, supercharge, harness, journey (unless literal), crucial, foster, vibrant, bespoke, synergy.
8. No bold run-in labels as filler.
9. No vague attributions ("studies show") without a named source.
10. No padding ("in order to", "whether you're X or Y").
11. Vary sentence length.
12. No invented numbers, testimonials or logos; missing facts become TODOs.

## 3. Length limits
Hero headline 3–8 words; subheadline 15–30 words; eyebrow 1–3 words; section heading 3–8 words; section intro ≤ 25 words; card title 2–5 words; card body 10–25 words; button 1–3 words, verb first; FAQ answer 20–60 words; meta title 50–60 characters; meta description 140–160 characters.

## 4. Formulas
Headlines: outcome + condition ("Verify once. Approve every share."); plain category + benefit ("Real food, delivered fresh."); benefits in real terms ("Large transactions, small fees, wide reach."); the user's own question ("What are you craving?").
Subheadline: "<Do the main action> once, then <control or result>, instead of <current pain>."
CTA: verb + object ("Join the waitlist", "Order now", "See how it works", "Book a demo"). Avoid "Submit", "Click here", and "Learn more" without an object. Pair the primary with a lower-commitment secondary.

## 5. Section templates
Hero: headline, subheadline, two CTAs, one proof line. How it works: "<Number> steps. <Pain removed>." with 01–03 verb titles and one sentence each. Value blocks: principle heading ("You decide what leaves your hands."), 3–4 claims with an example each. Proof: numbers with units and dates, named people with role and company. FAQ: customer wording; answer money, safety and availability directly. Final CTA: invitation + current status line. Footer: positioning line, grouped links, legal operator and registration numbers.

## 6. Microcopy
Labels are nouns; helper text says why. Errors say what happened and the fix ("Enter an email address like name@example.com."). Success sets expectations ("You're on the list. We'll email you when we open in your area."). Empty states name the space and the first action. Loading over 1s says what is loading. Destructive confirmations name the object ("Delete account" / "Keep account").

## 7. Do and don't
| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Unlock seamless identity verification. | Verify your identity once and reuse it. |
| Elevate your dining experience. | Order from kitchens near you and track the rider. |
| Trusted by thousands of happy customers. | A counted, dated, true number. |
| Get Started | Join the waitlist |

## 8. Accessible copy
Link text makes sense alone; alt text describes content (decorative images alt=""); no color-only instructions; grade 7–9 reading level for consumers.
